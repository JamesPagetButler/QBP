## Per-entry notes

**1. Kraemer et al., Nature 440, 315 (2006).** Fetched full text via ar5iv (cond-mat/0512394). Quoted: a₋ = −850(20) a₀ (three-body loss resonance / giant recombination loss); atom-dimer resonance a₊ = 1060(70) a₀; measured ratio a₊/|a₋| = 1.25(9) vs. universal-theory prediction 0.96(3). This is the founding experimental Efimov paper (Innsbruck/Grimm group). Fully verified against fetched text.

**2. Zaccanti et al., Nature Physics 5, 586 (2009).** Citation and general claim (two loss features, first evidence of an Efimov spectrum, direct test of the 22.7 scaling factor) confirmed via WebSearch summaries of the arXiv abstract (arXiv:0904.4453) and Nature Physics page. However, WebFetch could not render the PDF as text (binary/FlateDecode), and the abs page didn't carry the numeric values, so no scattering-length values with uncertainty were recovered. Needs a follow-up fetch of the actual figure/table data (or a copy of the PDF converted to text) before it can go on the ledger with numbers.

**3a/3b. Gross et al. PRL 103, 163202 (2009) and Pollack/Dries/Hulet Science 326, 1683 (2009).** Both fully verified via ar5iv full-text fetch. Gross: single recombination-minimum/Efimov-resonance pair (a₊=243(35)a₀, a₋=−264(11)a₀, ratio 0.92(14) vs. theory 0.96(3)). Pollack et al.: two trimers on each side of the resonance, with measured ratios a₂₊/a₁₊=22.5(22)(11) and a₂₋/a₁₋=21.1(11)(24) against the universal 22.7 — one of the cleanest scaling-factor confirmations in the literature, plus the four-body features noted in the abstract (11 total three+four-body features; not individually itemized here).

**4a/4b/4c. Li-6 three-component series.** Ottenstein et al. 2008 (PRL 101, 203202) is titled "Collisional Stability of a Three-Component Degenerate Fermi Gas" and its own abstract reports a loss feature at 130 G without invoking "Efimov" — the Efimov interpretation is retrospective (via Williams et al. 2009). Huckans et al. 2009 (PRL 102, 165302) abstract gives at = −2140 a₀ convergence and the ~5×10⁻²² cm⁶ s⁻¹ loss coefficient scale, with two unexpected resonant loss features. Williams et al. 2009 (PRL 103, 130404) abstract explicitly identifies an excited-state Efimov trimer near 895 G and retroactively assigns the 130 G and 500 G features to ground-state trimers via κ*/η* universal parameters. All three citations verified; numeric detail is as good as each paper's own abstract provides (fine structure inside the papers not extracted).

**5a/5b. Lompe et al. Science 330, 940 (2010); Nakajima et al. PRL 106, 143201 (2011).** Citations confirmed (RF association technique used to directly measure Efimov trimer binding energies in Li-6). Neither abstract, as rendered by the fetch tools available, yielded a specific binding-energy number with an error bar — flagged UNVERIFIED pending a proper full-text pull.

**6. Van der Waals universality package (Berninger 2011 / Chin 2011 / Wang-D'Incao-Esry-Greene 2012 / Schmidt-Rath-Zwerger 2012).** This cluster is the most citation-solid but numerically weakest part of the sweep: WebFetch repeatedly failed to render the Berninger PDF (arXiv:1106.3933) as text, so the widely-quoted a₋ ≈ −9.7 r_vdW figure could only be corroborated via search-engine synthesis of citing works (which is why it's flagged PARTIALLY VERIFIED, with one secondary source instead giving ≈−9.5 r_vdW — the discrepancy itself should be resolved against the primary PDF before ledger entry). Chin's arXiv:1111.1484 abstract synthesis gives C = −a₋¹/r_vdW ≈ 8.5–9.5 across species (K-39 excepted) and an analytic C=9.475 from a Born–Oppenheimer/quantum-reflection model — again from search synthesis, not a raw abstract quote I directly captured. The two 2012 theory papers (Wang et al. PRL 108, 263001; Schmidt-Rath & Zwerger EPJB 85, 386) are confirmed as the theoretical explanations of this universality; no new experimental numbers apply to them.

**7. Huang, Sidorenkov, Grimm, Hutson, PRL 112, 190401 (2014).** IMPORTANT CORRECTION to the target list: the paper testing the 22.7 scaling with three consecutive Cs Efimov states is PRL **112, 190401** (arXiv:1402.6161), not "113, 240402" as given in the brief — 113,240402 is Tung et al.'s Li-Cs paper (row 8b). Verified measured scaling factor: 21.0(1.3) for the second/first resonance ratio in identical-boson Cs, compared to the ideal 22.7.

**8a/8b/8c. Heteronuclear Li-Cs series.** Pires et al. PRL 112, 250404 (2014) and Tung et al. PRL 113, 240402 (2014) both report multiple (2–3) consecutive heteronuclear Efimov resonances with scaling "close to" the universal 4.9 for this mass ratio (~22.7 for Li/Cs mass ratio), but I could not extract the specific measured λ value with its uncertainty from either paper's abstract/PDF in this session (flagged accordingly). Ulmanis et al. PRL 117, 153201 (2016) abstract *does* give a clean number: scaling factor 4.0(3), a significant deviation from 4.9 attributed to the Cs-Cs intraspecies scattering length — fully verified.

**9. Kunitski et al., Science 348, 551 (2015).** Direct real-space imaging of the He-4 excited Efimov trimer via Coulomb explosion imaging (COLTRIMS). Verified citation and the qualitative structural result (huge trimer, >100 Å scale, non-isosceles acute triangle geometry) from search summaries of the Science abstract and coverage; did not recover the specific mean bond-length value with its quoted uncertainty (would require the actual PDF/table).

**10a/10b. Roy et al. PRL 111, 053202 (2013); Wild et al. PRL 108, 145305 (2012).** Roy et al. (K-39, narrow Feshbach resonances) confirmed as testing universality at narrow resonances, finding unexpectedly universal behavior; Wild et al.'s Rb-85 paper is primarily about Tan's contact but does contain an Efimov resonance location within it per its abstract description in search results — flagged UNVERIFIED for exact numbers pending full-text access.

**11a/11b. Four-body tetramers.** Ferlaino et al. (PRL 102, 140401, 2009) confirmed as the discovery paper for Cs tetramers tied to the Efimov trimer. The specific "universal ratios 0.43 and 0.9" named in the brief were NOT found in this sweep — what I could confirm instead (from von Stecher, D'Incao, Greene, Nature Physics 5, 417, 2009) are the theoretical binding-energy ratios B₄⁽⁰⁾≈4.57 B₃ and B₄⁽¹⁾≈1.01 B₃, which are a different (though related) universal statement. Do not enter "0.43/0.9" into the ledger without locating their exact source — they may refer to a scattering-length-ratio convention (a₄/a₃) reported inside the PRL 102,140401 full text that I could not access as machine-readable text this session.

**12. Nuclear and 2023–2026 developments.** No experimentally confirmed Efimov effect exists in nuclear physics: the triton (only three-nucleon bound state with no excited state) is used purely as a theoretical illustration of the underlying universal correlations (Phillips line), and current halo-nuclide work (e.g. arXiv:2606.19231) is Faddeev/EFT theory, not measurement. For heteronuclear dipolar mixtures (Er-Li, Dy-Li), quantum-degenerate mixtures have now been realized (arXiv:2506.00177) and Efimov-state predictions published (arXiv:2111.08130, arXiv:2506.07721), but no experimental Efimov-resonance observation in these systems was located in this sweep — this looks like an open/pending result, worth a dedicated follow-up search closer to publication dates. Photonic/synthetic-lattice "Efimov-like" analogues (e.g. hyperbolic-surface lattices, Science Bulletin 2021) and the observed 2D–1D dimensional crossover in ultracold bosons (arXiv:2308.00411) are adjacent but are not themselves Efimov-resonance measurements.

## Known limitations of this sweep

- WebFetch in this environment reliably renders **arXiv abstract pages** and some ar5iv HTML renders, but frequently fails to extract text from **arXiv PDF downloads** (returns raw/binary notice) and from some APS/journal full-text mirrors. This limited how many papers could be checked past the abstract level.
- Several "measured value ± error" cells above are therefore only as precise as what each paper's own abstract states; internal-text numbers (tables, figures) were not always reachable.
- The "0.43 / 0.9" tetramer ratios and the exact Zaccanti/Tung/Pires/Roy/Wild/Kunitski/Lompe/Nakajima numeric values should be treated as **open items** — re-run with direct PDF-to-text access (or Nature/Science/APS full-text scraping with credentials) before committing exact numbers to the physics ledger.
