# Research Note: Tim Maudlin on Bell's Theorem (Curt Jaimungal / TOE, "Quantum Nonlocality Explained FROM SCRATCH")

## 1. Twelve-bullet outline

1. **EPR completeness question**: not "is QM correct?" but "is the wave function complete?" — reality criterion: certain prediction without disturbance ⇒ an element of reality exists.
2. **Locality**, not determinism, is EPR's real premise: space-like separation is *assumed* to rule out disturbance. Determinism is a derived theorem, not a starting assumption.
3. Einstein's objection to Copenhagen was **non-locality**, not indeterminism and not superluminal *signaling* — he never claimed QM permits signaling.
4. **Bohm (1951)** reframed EPR as a **spin** (Stern-Gerlach) case — logically identical, but opens infinitely many possible settings instead of two.
5. Pilot-wave theory is **deterministic and explicitly non-local** (Bob's outcome counterfactually depends on Alice's distant choice) yet still cannot signal — non-locality ≠ signaling.
6. **Bell (1964)**: does any *local* theory reproduce QM's correlation statistics at three magnet-angle offsets (100%/75%/25% disagreement at 0°/60°/120°)?
7. **The proof** (instruction-set table, after Nick Herbert): any local assignment matching the 0°/60° cases forces ≥50% disagreement at 120°, but QM/experiment needs 25% — contradiction.
8. Bell's argument rests on **exactly two assumptions**: (i) locality and (ii) **statistical independence** (settings uncorrelated with the source — the "free variables" assumption).
9. Given (i)+(ii): **"locality is dead."** Clauser/Aspect/Zeilinger experiments (Nobel 2022) confirm the QM statistics.
10. Many-worlds, pressed as a possible local escape, is conceded even by proponents to be "massively non-local" once decoherence's global character is examined.
11. Conway-Kochen "Free Will Theorem" is dismissed as relabeling adding nothing beyond Bell — "free will" framing called a category error.
12. **Verdict**: the only remaining escape is denying statistical independence (superdeterminism, unnamed) — logically possible but rejected as unreasonable, since it would undercut all controlled-experiment methodology.

## 3. The video's recommendation for deciding whether a kill is real

Explicit and methodical, though never in these words:
- **Reduce to the minimal, named assumption set** — Bell's proof is trusted because it rests on exactly two independently checkable premises, not tacit ones.
- **Read the actual proof, not a secondhand report** — the von Neumann kill survived ~30 years on hearsay/translation; once someone (Hermann, later Bell) checked the operator-linearity step itself, it collapsed.
- **Demand ensemble/statistical confirmation, not a single run** — the proof is a repeatable, positive-measure statistical claim (75%/25% over many trials), checked against real Clauser/Aspect/Zeilinger data.
- **Separate "logically possible" from "reasonable"** — routes like denying statistical independence aren't automatically live; the test is whether the escape destroys the methodology used to justify anything, including itself.

## 4. Mapping onto QBP's 2026-09-25 lesson

The von Neumann case is the direct analogue: a "ruled out mathematically" kill stood unchallenged for decades not because it was re-derived, but because nobody re-examined the assumption it rested on — exactly QBP's pattern of a kill withdrawn as *never proven*, with the surrounding conclusion separately unreversed once Bell supplied an actual, assumption-minimal proof. Bell's proof is itself the good-kill exemplar QBP wants: a positive-measure statistical claim over many trials at fixed, reachable angle offsets, not one that survives only because a configuration structurally cannot be reached.
